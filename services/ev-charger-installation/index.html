EV charger installation in Windsor.

Choosing between a plug-in and a hardwired charger for your Windsor home affects the receptacle, the circuit and the paperwork. An insurer or lender may ask which was installed and by whom.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Windsor property

Note where the vehicle parks, how far that is from the existing panel and whether the route crosses finished rooms. Collect the charger's product sheet, which states how the unit is meant to connect.

Ask whether the existing receptacle location, if any, is suitable at all, or whether a dedicated circuit is needed either way. Ask how the installation will be recorded so a later insurer or lender question can be answered.

What should the assessment establish?

The charger chosen for a Windsor driveway or garage may need either a plug-in connection on a receptacle or a hardwired one on its own circuit; the report names the one that fits, with reasons from the location, exposure and expected use. The report also sets out what the existing supply can carry alongside the other equipment in the home. The scope lists the approved equipment, the circuit and the ESA notification. It also describes the record of the work you receive, for a lender or insurer to read.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is a plug-in charger easier to document than a hardwired one?

Neither is automatically easier. A plug-in unit still needs a suitable receptacle and circuit, and a hardwired unit needs its own connection. Ask the contractor which arrangement the installation record will describe and why it was chosen.

Should I tell the insurer before I add a charger to the house?

Policies differ, so read yours and ask the insurer directly. A lender may have its own conditions as well. Keep the product sheet, the ESA notification and the contractor's written scope ready in case they ask.
