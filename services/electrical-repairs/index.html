A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Windsor property

Write down which rooms or buildings lost power, what was running and whether a labelled breaker looks different from the others. Do not open the panel or reset anything repeatedly, and keep a dated note of what you saw.

Ask whether the contractor can tell which circuits feed the affected area when earlier alterations left no labels. Ask what the written findings will say if a cause cannot be confirmed on the first visit.

What should the assessment establish?

The symptom as you described it heads the report, with the scope of what was examined beside it. The next part shows how the affected area is supplied, which may hold a surprise in a Windsor house if earlier work added a feeder nobody recorded. One line then names the cause found, or says plainly that none was confirmed. Repairs that restore the circuit sit in a list of their own, apart from any wider concerns noticed along the way. If an insurer's or lender's question concerns the event, the report describes it in neutral terms.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

What should I tell the contractor when only the garage has lost power?

Say when it happened, what was running and whether anything unusual was noticed, such as a smell or buzzing. Leave the equipment alone, and do not open the panel; diagnosis belongs to the contractor.

Will the fault assessment identify who did earlier wiring changes?

Not usually. It describes what is found, and the installer of an older change often cannot be known. Invoices and ESA paperwork you collected may name earlier contractors, so hand them over with your account of the fault.

Prepare your enquiry

Useful project details

  • Observed symptom and when it began
  • Rooms or devices affected
  • Equipment operating at the time
  • Recent alterations and previous repairs
  • Owner or manager who can authorize access and repairs

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
