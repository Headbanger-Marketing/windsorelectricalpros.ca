Before work begins

Match your records to what the request asks.

Without records, hidden wiring can be hard to account for. An older house that has had rooms added, circuits extended and a panel changed over the years may hold wiring of different ages and methods, with no paperwork saying who made each change.

Ask the insurer or lender to state in writing exactly what it needs, then gather photographs of labels, past invoices and any earlier ESA paperwork. A contractor can then say which parts of that request an assessment can answer.

What counts as a record when the insurer asks about my wiring?

The insurer should say which documents it accepts. Possible items include ESA paperwork from earlier work, invoices naming the contractor, product sheets and dated photographs of the panel door and its labels. Gaps are worth stating plainly rather than guessing.

Can an assessment report answer a lender's question about old wiring?

It can if the scope is written to match the question. Ask the lender what form it accepts, then ask the contractor whether the written result will cover those items, and what it cannot see behind finished walls.

How do I compare two quotes when the request letter is vague?

Give each contractor the same copy of the letter and the same photographs. Then compare what each scope includes, what it excludes and which items the written result is meant to address for the reader of the letter.

Who needs to agree before work starts if a lender holds the mortgage?

Check the mortgage or policy terms and ask the lender or insurer whether it must be told about changes. The owner authorizes the work, and the contractor explains the ESA notification, while any building permit is a separate matter.

Does sending the form mean a contractor will answer my lender's letter?

No. A provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor, if you hire one, proves its identity and licence, and agrees with you directly on the job's extent and on its price and dates.
